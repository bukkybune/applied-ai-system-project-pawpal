# Health, medication and parasites

## Giving medication
Give medication exactly as your vet prescribed: the same dose, at evenly spaced times,
for the full course, even if your pet seems better. Medicines meant to be given "twice
daily" are usually spaced about 12 hours apart. Ask your vet before stopping a medicine
or changing a dose.

## Human medicines are dangerous for pets
Never give a pet human painkillers or cold medicines unless a vet tells you to.
Ibuprofen, naproxen and paracetamol (acetaminophen) can cause serious poisoning in dogs
and cats; paracetamol is especially dangerous for cats.

## Fleas, ticks and worms
Regular parasite prevention protects pets and the people they live with. Many products
are given monthly, but the right product and schedule depend on your pet's species,
weight and lifestyle, so ask your vet which one to use. Never use a dog flea product on a
cat; some contain permethrin, which is toxic to cats.

## Itching and scratching
Frequent scratching, licking or chewing at the skin can come from fleas, allergies, skin
infections or ear problems. Head shaking and scratching at one ear can point to an ear
infection or ear mites. These need a vet to diagnose; do not put anything inside the ear
canal without advice.

## When to call a vet
Contact a vet if your pet stops eating for more than a day, has repeated vomiting or
diarrhoea, seems in pain, is lethargic, drinks or urinates much more than usual, or
loses weight without explanation. You know your pet best; if something seems wrong, ask.
