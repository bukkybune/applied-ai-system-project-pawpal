# Cat care basics

## Daily needs of adult cats
Adult cats usually do well on two or more measured meals a day, fresh water every day and
a clean litter box. Cats are good at hiding illness, so small changes in eating,
drinking or litter habits are worth noticing.

## Litter box care
Scoop the litter box at least once a day and fully replace the litter regularly. A common
guideline is one litter box per cat plus one extra. A cat that suddenly avoids the litter
box may be stressed or unwell.

## Play and exercise for cats
Indoor cats need daily play to stay fit and avoid boredom. Two or more short sessions a
day with a wand toy or chase game, around 10 to 15 minutes each, suit most cats. Puzzle
feeders and climbing spaces add enrichment.

## Water and urinary health
Encourage drinking with fresh water every day; some cats prefer a fountain or a bowl away
from their food. A male cat straining in the litter box and producing little or no urine
is an emergency and needs a vet straight away.

## Senior cats
Cats are often considered senior from around 11 years old. Watch for weight loss,
drinking more, changes in appetite or litter habits, and stiffness, and mention them to a
vet. Many vets suggest check-ups every six months for older cats.

## Grooming cats
Most short-haired cats groom themselves but enjoy a weekly brush; long-haired cats need
brushing several times a week to prevent mats. Older or overweight cats may struggle to
groom and need more help.
