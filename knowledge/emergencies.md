# Emergencies and toxic foods

## Signs of an emergency
Go to a vet or an emergency clinic straight away if a pet has trouble breathing,
collapses, has a seizure, is bleeding heavily, may have broken a bone, has been hit by a
car, has a swollen hard belly with unproductive retching, cannot urinate, or may have
eaten something poisonous. Call ahead if you can so the clinic can prepare.

## Foods toxic to dogs
Chocolate, grapes and raisins, xylitol (a sweetener in some sugar-free gum, sweets and
peanut butter), onions, garlic, macadamia nuts and alcohol are toxic to dogs. Cooked
bones can splinter and cause injury.

## Foods and plants toxic to cats
Onions, garlic, chocolate, alcohol, grapes and raisins are toxic to cats. Lilies are
extremely dangerous to cats: eating any part of the plant, or even drinking water from a
vase of lilies, can cause kidney failure.

## Heat
Dogs and cats can overheat quickly in hot cars, hot weather or after hard exercise. Never
leave a pet in a parked car. Walk dogs in the cooler parts of the day in hot weather and
check pavement temperature with the back of your hand.

## If your pet may have been poisoned
Do not wait for symptoms. Contact a vet or a pet poison helpline straight away, keep the
packaging of whatever was eaten, and do not make your pet vomit unless a vet tells you
to.
