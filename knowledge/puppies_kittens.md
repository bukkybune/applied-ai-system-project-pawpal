# Puppies and kittens

## Feeding young animals
Puppies and kittens have small stomachs and high energy needs, so they are usually fed
three to four small meals a day, moving to two meals as they grow (often around six
months, later for large-breed puppies). Use food made for their life stage and follow
the feeding guide on the label or your vet's advice.

## Toilet training puppies
Take a puppy outside after every meal, after waking up, after play and every couple of
hours in between. Praise or reward straight after they go in the right place. Accidents
are normal; punishment makes training slower.

## Exercise for puppies
Puppies need several short, gentle play and walk sessions rather than long hikes. Their
joints are still developing, so avoid long runs, repeated jumping and forced exercise,
particularly with large breeds. Let them rest and nap often.

## Training and socialisation
Short training sessions of 5 to 10 minutes, a few times a day, work best for young
puppies. Calm, positive introductions to people, sounds, surfaces and other vaccinated
animals during the first months help them grow into confident adults.

## Kittens
Kittens learn to use a litter box quickly, usually from their mother. Give them several
short play sessions a day and get them used to gentle handling of paws, ears and mouth so
grooming and vet visits are easier later.

## Vaccinations and first vet visit
Young animals need a course of vaccinations, deworming and a general health check. The
schedule depends on the animal, the vaccines used and local risks, so book a vet visit
soon after bringing a new puppy or kitten home and follow the vet's plan.
